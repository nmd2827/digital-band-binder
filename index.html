How the Book Is Built Rush & Scott · GIA

The method opens with a 10-part Pre-Book Sequence — steady beat, rhythm charts, and solfege (Do, Re, Mi…) learned before anyone touches an instrument, so no book is needed for the first lessons. Then come the units: nine 20-line units, one 10-line unit, and a closing 6-line scale unit — 22 of the lines are marked playing tests. First sounds start on the “small instrument” — mouthpiece, mouthpiece and barrel, reed, bocal and reed, or headjoint — before the first full-instrument notes in Unit 1.

  • Solfege from day one — every line is sung before it is played
  • Rhythm first, then pitches — each rhythm chart returns later with notes on it, so reading transfers straight into the music
  • Rhythm vocabulary, in order: quarter notes → eighth notes → dotted rhythms → sixteenth notes → an eighth and two sixteenths, with all matching rests
  • Five keys: Concert B♭, E♭, A♭, C, and F — the Concert G scale is printed in the back of the book

Full details and previews of the clarinet, trumpet, and percussion books are on the publisher’s page: GIA Publications — Habits of a Successful Beginner Band Musician.

Read This First — How the Weeks Are Paced Honest pacing note

GIA does not publish a week-by-week calendar for this book, and the full method is designed to take about a year and a half to two years to finish — it is not a one-semester book. So the 18 weeks below are this binder’s pacing of the book’s own order, not a publisher schedule: 2 weeks of Pre-Book, then Units 1–5 (Lines 1–100) at three weeks per unit, closing with a playing-test week. That stopping point matches how experienced Habits directors pace year one — Unit 5 (Line 100) is a common end-of-year target. Line numbers, page numbers, unit breaks, and playing-test lines below are the book’s own, taken from its published unit inventory. Units 6–11 are listed at the bottom for next semester.
